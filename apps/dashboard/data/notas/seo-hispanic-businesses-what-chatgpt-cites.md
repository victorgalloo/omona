# SEO for Hispanic businesses in the US: which pages ChatGPT cites when an owner asks who can rank them

**Short answer:** on November 27, 2026 we asked ChatGPT the question a business owner would ask: who can get me found on Google and on ChatGPT. In Spanish, it cited **11 agency pages**. We read each one. Almost none of them has Google traffic: the 11 sites combined get about **28 estimated visits a month**. What they share is shape. The page says who it serves in the title, answers specific questions, and declares its facts in a way a machine can read. SEO is Google. GEO is the AI assistants. Today the same page wins both.

![What the 11 agency pages ChatGPT cited have in common](/blog/seo-hispanic-businesses-what-chatgpt-cites/pages-cited-by-chatgpt.svg)

## 1. What we asked and what it answered

Three questions to ChatGPT with web search on, asked once each:

| Question | What it cited |
|---|---|
| In Spanish: which agency or tool do you recommend in Mexico so my business shows up on ChatGPT, Gemini and Google? | 5 Mexican agency pages and 1 media review |
| In Spanish: I own a Hispanic business in the United States. Who can rank me on Google and ChatGPT, in Spanish and English? | 6 bilingual agency pages and 2 Google help pages |
| In English: what is the best AI SEO tool or service to get my small business recommended by ChatGPT and ranked on Google? | No agencies: only Google and OpenAI documentation, and it named well-known SEO tools |

Omona was not named in any of the three. That is why we measured.

One useful detail: the answer shows what ChatGPT searched before replying. For each Spanish question it ran **one or two agency searches** in plain words (for the US one: "bilingual Hispanic SEO agency United States local SEO Spanish English") and spent the rest on official documentation. The cited pages are the ones that answer that plain search from the title.

## 2. What the 11 cited pages have in common

We downloaded each page and counted what can be verified in its code:

| Trait of the page | Pages | Of 11 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Declares the business in structured data (Organization, LocalBusiness or ProfessionalService) | 11 | 100% |
| Marks up its FAQ (FAQPage) | 9 | 82% |
| Title of 60 characters or fewer | 9 | 82% |
| Declares its offer in structured data (Offer) | 8 | 73% |
| Declares a version in another language (hreflang) | 7 | 64% |
| One-tap phone (`tel:` link) | 7 | 64% |
| WhatsApp link | 6 | 55% |

Four more facts:

- **They are service pages, not articles.** None of the 11 is a blog post.
- **The title repeats the question.** It names the service, who it is for and where: SEO, Hispanic businesses, the United States or the city.
- **All 6 US pages declare their version in the other language.** Of the 5 Mexican ones, only 1 does.
- **They are not long.** The median is about 1,240 words across the whole page.

## 3. What they do not have: Google traffic

We checked how many Google searches bring visits to each of the 11 sites in its own country:

- The 11 combined show up for **63 searches** and get about **28 estimated visits a month**.
- The largest gets about **16 a month**. Two do not show up for any measured search.

In other words, being cited for a question this specific did not take being big on Google. It took having the exact page. That is good news for a small business and a warning for anyone buying "more articles": volume is not what decided it here.

## 4. What to check on your page this week

- **One page per service**, with the service, your customer and your area in the title, in 60 characters or fewer.
- **The questions people ask you on the phone, answered on the page** in two or three lines each, and marked up as an FAQ.
- **Your business facts in structured data:** name, area served, phone, hours.
- **Spanish and English as two sister pages** that declare each other, not a machine translation layered on top.
- **Text that reads without JavaScript.** Assistant crawlers generally do not run code.
- **Crawlers allowed in:** make sure your `robots.txt` does not block the ones from OpenAI, Anthropic, Perplexity or Google.
- **A way to answer fast.** Being found is worth little if the lead writes and nobody replies: measure your first response with the [free speed-to-lead checker](/en/speed-to-lead-checker).

## 5. Limits of this measurement

- It is **one day, one assistant and three questions**. Assistant answers change between runs and between assistants.
- These are **traits of the cited pages, not proven causes**. We did not measure similar pages that were not cited.
- Visits are **estimates** from an SEO tool based on the searches where each site appears, not data from their analytics.
- We publish aggregates only. We do not name the agencies.

We left questions on this topic on weekly tracking across ChatGPT, Gemini and Google.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is SEO for Hispanic businesses?** Getting your business to show up when a customer searches for your service in Spanish or English, on Google and Google Maps. For a Hispanic-owned business in the US it usually means working both languages.

**How is GEO different from SEO?** SEO is Google. GEO is the AI assistants: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini. The assistant searches the web, reads a handful of pages and cites the ones that answer the question best.

**Can you pay to appear in ChatGPT?** ChatGPT's own answer warned against anyone who promises a guaranteed spot or says the assistant can be paid to recommend you. What is in your hands is making your page the one that answers best.

**Do I need to publish a lot of articles?** Not in this measurement. None of the 11 cited pages was an article, and the sites have almost no Google traffic. Start with one page per service; then add content with your own data.

**Do I need my site in Spanish and English?** If you serve customers in both, yes. All 6 pages cited for the US question declare their version in the other language.
